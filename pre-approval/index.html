The Debt-To-Income Ratio (DTI)

Your buying power largely depends on your verifiable income relative to your monthly debt obligations.

  • Gross Income: Lenders use your gross (pre-tax) income to calculate affordability.
  • DTI Ratio: Your total monthly debt payments (including the new mortgage) will be limited to 43-50% of your gross monthly income for most programs. VA Loans and other specialty loans do offer more aggressive ratios.
  • Variable Income: Bonuses, Overtime, RSU, Self-Employed, and tax-free income involve more complex analyses. Team Insider specializes in analyzing various income methods and sources to reach your financial goals.

CASH TO CLOSE

You need cash to close, which is broken down into 4 categories:

  • 1. Down Payment: Minimums range from 0% (of the purchase price) (VA/USDA/DPA) to 3% or 3.5% (FHA/Conventional) to any higher percent. Down Payments greater than 20% eliminates possible Mortgage Insurance (MI).
  • 2. Transaction Closing Costs: These are fees paid to third parties directly related to the transaction (such as escrow fees, title insurance, appraisal, credit, lender, county recording fees AND cost of the rate). Note, the cost of the rate could result in an offset (credit) towards the closing costs.
  • 3. Prepaids: Hazard Insurance Premiums, Prorated Property Taxes, Prepaid Interest to the lender, HOA dues, and a possible property tax and/or insurance IMPOUND RESERVE are items where cash is needed in escrow but are not a cost of the transaction.
  • 4. Reserves: Depending on the type of loan, lenders may require you to have extra cash left over after closing (usually equivalent to several months of mortgage payments). These funds are not to be used as collateral; the lender just needs to know they exist.
